# Battery OEM shipping

Lead-acid batteries are Class 8 corrosive dangerous goods. OEM shipping is a documentation and packaging discipline as much as a logistics one — get the classification and paperwork right and the container moves; miss it and it sits at the port.

## Dangerous-goods classification

Wet lead-acid batteries are **Class 8 (corrosive)** under the IMDG Code:

- **UN 2794** — batteries, wet, filled with acid.
- **UN 2800** — batteries, wet, non-spillable (where the battery meets the vibration and pressure-differential tests).

Unlike lithium (Class 9), lead-acid needs no UN 38.3 report — but it still needs Class 8 labelling, compliant packaging and, where applicable, a dangerous-goods declaration.

## Packaging

Terminals protected against shorting, upright orientation, export-grade pallets sized to the container plan, and mixed-model loading where agreed.

## Export documentation

At minimum: commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin and a dangerous-goods declaration — plus test reports and MSDS where the destination requires them.

## Incoterms and freight

Shipment is made under the Incoterms agreed in writing (FOB, CFR or CIF). Production lead time is 30–45 days; ocean freight and clearance add 3–5 weeks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are lead-acid batteries dangerous goods to ship?

Yes — Class 8 corrosive. Flooded starting batteries ship as UN 2794; non-spillable designs as UN 2800. Unlike lithium, no UN 38.3 test report is required.

### What documents does a battery shipment need?

At least five: commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin and a dangerous-goods declaration — plus test reports and MSDS where required.

### Which Incoterms do you ship under?

FOB, CFR or CIF, as agreed in writing. Risk transfers at the point defined by the agreed Incoterm.

### How long does shipping take?

Production lead time is 30–45 days; add 3–5 weeks for ocean freight and clearance.
